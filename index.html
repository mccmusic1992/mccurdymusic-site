About

01 — Bio

Full Bio

The long version.

Chris McCurdy is serving his sixth year as the Band Director at Grant High School in Portland, Oregon. At Grant, he teaches two levels of concert band, a jazz improvisation lab, and three levels of jazz ensemble. Seasonally, he directs the symphony orchestra, pit orchestra, and athletic bands. Prior to joining the staff at Grant, Mr. McCurdy taught for four years at Lakeridge High School and Lakeridge Middle School in Lake Oswego, Oregon.

As a performer, Chris can be found playing saxophone for various big bands and funk groups in Portland. He is a graduate of the University of Oregon, B.Mus, Music Education; M.Mus, Music Education, studying conducting with Dr. Rodney Dorsey and pedagogy with Dr. Eric Wiltshire. He was recently recognized as a member of the 2025 Yamaha "40 Under 40" list, celebrating excellence in music education.

In his free time, Chris can be found biking or camping around the Northwest with his dog Basie and eating his way through the Portland food scene with his partner Jacquelyn.

Teaching Philosophy

A few things I believe about teaching music.

In no particular order.

Access

Every student deserves a way in

No matter where or when a student starts, I try to keep as many doors open as I can — non-audition entry points, flexible pacing, and enough fundraising that cost is never the reason a student sits out of a festival or honor band.

Ownership

It's fine to take a wrong turn

This shows up most in jazz. I'd rather a student take a chance in a solo and land somewhere unexpected than stick to the safe notes. Confidence comes from doing it wrong a few hundred times — that's just how it works, and I try to make room for it in lessons.

Community

Students teach students

The strongest programs I've been part of weren't running on top-down instruction alone — older students were teaching younger ones almost as much as I was teaching either of them. I build that in on purpose now, pairing upperclassmen with freshmen so the culture carries itself forward each year instead of resetting every fall.
